## Abstract

Tidal stresses in California reach only about 0.6 kPa in the semidiurnal band, and rate-and-state friction predicts a seismicity-rate modulation of order 1 percent at such amplitudes (Beeler and Lockner, 2003), which is small enough that most published searches are limited by sample size rather than by physics. Sirorattanakul and Avouac (2026) report no statistically significant semidiurnal tidal modulation in either Northern or Southern California and bound the zero-to-peak amplitude below 8.16 percent of the background rate using a closed-form Schuster detection threshold. We ask a different and narrower question about a specific experiment: given a pre-registered, hash-frozen tidal-phase forecasting design on Southern California SCSN events at M greater than or equal to 1.5 over 1981 to 2018, focal-mechanism-matched and stress-resolved, what modulation amplitude would that design have recovered 80 percent of the time, with its data-driven cell-selection step simulated end to end?

We answer by injection and recovery through the full analysis pipeline, and we verify the simulator before we read it: at zero injected amplitude the empirical false-positive rate lies inside a binomial 99 percent acceptance band of [0.015, 0.095] in 13 of 13 configurations, and that arm was required to pass before any power arm was read. The resulting minimum detectable amplitudes at 80 percent power, after a measured and bias-audited correction for clustering-driven variance inflation, are 6.6 percent pooled over all 42 eligible bins (n = 3,920, conservative reading), 8.5 percent on the 22 anti-leak control bins (n = 1,906), about 10 percent through the complete end-to-end pipeline including selection (n = 3,920), and 25.3 percent and 43.8 percent for the two single-bin designs (n = 245 and n = 113). A full-catalogue intensity-likelihood configuration reaches 3.0 percent at n = 23,465, but we do not quote it as a stand-alone limit, because its own off-tidal negative-control line fails at that sample size (rejection rate 0.34 against a nominal 0.05), which is the clustering-driven p-value drift that Ader and Avouac (2013) named and that motivated their Schuster spectrum. No configuration contacts the 1 percent theory line. The corresponding null result, that static tidal-phase susceptibility maps show no out-of-sample forecasting skill in this region and window, is therefore a corpse of the map and not of the physics, and it is a rediscovery in a new geometry rather than a new claim: Hirose, Maeda and Kamigaichi (2022) scored a tidal-correlation forecast model on a Molchan error diagram and found it as ineffective as random guessing at Mw greater than or equal to 7.0, and Wang and Shearer (2015) found low-p tidal cells in central Japan no more numerous than chance.

Our bound is tighter than, and consistent with, the published California and Nepal bounds, and it is obtained by an independent route: evaluating the Sirorattanakul and Avouac closed form at our own sample sizes and our own multiplicity reproduces our simulated values to within a factor of 1.7 on every configuration, and the residual is quantitatively the 50-percent-power to 80-percent-power conversion factor that a Schuster statistic requires. The transferable contribution is a procedure rather than a physical result: report the minimum detectable amplitude at a stated power, computed through the selection step, with the simulator's false-positive rate verified at zero injected amplitude.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The two camps, in order, without editorialising

Solid-Earth and ocean tides load faults at amplitudes of order 1 kPa, which is small compared with earthquake stress drops but is measurable, periodic, and known in advance. That combination has made tidal triggering one of seismology's longest-running test problems. The positive line of evidence includes Tanaka, Ohtake and Sato (2002), Cochran, Vidale and Tanaka (2004), Metivier et al. (2009), Ide, Yabe and Tanaka (2016), and, for the Coso geothermal field specifically, Wang, Shearer, Vidale, Xu, Trugman and Fialko (2022), whose Figure 4c supplies one of the configurations priced below. At the low-stress-drop end of the spectrum the physics is not in dispute at all: tremor and low-frequency earthquakes respond to tidal stresses at amplitudes well below 1 kPa (Thomas, Nadeau and Burgmann, 2009; Thomas et al., 2012), which establishes that some part of the fault system is tidally sensitive and moves the question from whether to where, and at what amplitude.

The null line is equally long. Vidale, Agnew, Johnston and Oppenheimer (1998) found a rate ratio of about +2 percent on 13,042 Southern California events on the San Andreas and Calaveras systems, not statistically significant, which is an informal upper bound of exactly the kind this paper formalises. Wang and Shearer (2015) ran Schuster tests over spatiotemporal cells in central Japan at 0.2, 0.5 and 1.0 degrees crossed with 100, 200 and 400 day windows, and found low-p cells no more numerous than chance, which is the structural twin of the 42-bin scan described below and which diagnosed the grid-selection sensitivity that motivates our power calculation. Hough (2018) and Kossobokov and Panza (2020) are in the same line. Most importantly for this paper, Sirorattanakul and Avouac (2026) analysed California with the NCSS and Yang-Hauksson-Shearer catalogues over 2006 to 2021, on declustered background seismicity above the local completeness magnitude in 5,000-nearest-earthquake neighbourhoods, and reported no statistically significant semidiurnal tidal modulation together with a closed-form amplitude bound. The replication status of the positive claims has also been examined in public, in a DOI-bearing but not peer-reviewed replication essay (Bradley and Hubbard, 2024), which we cite as what it is.

### 1.2 The forecast-skill gap, and why it is thin rather than empty

Detection and forecasting are different questions. A statistically significant tidal modulation in a retrospective window does not imply that a susceptibility map built from that window has any predictive value in a later one. Exactly one prior scored tidal forecast model was located in an adversarial search: Hirose, Maeda and Kamigaichi (2022) built a model on the temporal variation of the tidal correlation, scored it on a Molchan error diagram, and reported that for Mw greater than or equal to 7.0 the model was "as ineffective as random guessing", with the best case at lower magnitudes reaching a probability gain of only about 1.7 in the Tonga-Kermadec region. Our own geometry is different (a static spatial susceptibility map, trained, hash-frozen, and scored out of sample on a strictly later window, with an anti-leak control), and the result is complementary to theirs rather than novel with respect to it.

### 1.3 What this paper adds, stated narrowly

The prior work described above establishes both halves of what follows. Minimum detectable amplitudes for periodic seismicity modulation are reported analytically in this literature, with a closed-form estimator (Ader and Avouac, 2013; Sirorattanakul and Avouac, 2026). The sensitivity of tidal p-values to the choice of spatiotemporal cell is documented (Wang and Shearer, 2015), and the general trials-factor formalism is standard (Gross and Vitells, 2010). This paper combines the two, by computing the minimum detectable amplitude at a stated statistical power through a simulation of the complete pipeline, including the data-driven selection of the reported cell, and by verifying the simulator's own false-positive rate at zero injected amplitude before any power result is read. We claim that combination and nothing else. In particular we do not claim the concept of reporting a bound in place of a null, which is at least thirteen years old in this literature and older elsewhere, and we do not claim the California tidal bound, which is Sirorattanakul and Avouac's.

We also report two things that work against us and that we found ourselves: our largest-n configuration fails its own off-tidal negative control, and our phase-clock convention has a measured non-uniform null response, since calibrated on real modelled tidal series under the frozen protocol of section 5.4 (medians 18.39 and 20.75 percent quarter-anchored; see section 2.7).

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Data, and the frozen protocol

Target catalogue: SCSN Southern California seismicity at M greater than or equal to 1.5 over 1981 to 2018, declustered, focal-mechanism-matched, with tidal shear stress resolved onto the mechanism plane following the section 2.5 convention of Lu, Xue, Yue, Zhuang and Zhao (2025). The focal-mechanism catalogue is the Yang-Hauksson-Shearer catalogue distributed through SCEDC. The declustered working file used by the power audit is `SCSN_decluster_m1.5.txt`, containing 23,465 focal-mechanism-matched events inside the analysis box, organised into 2,332 orientation groups over 42 eligible 0.4-degree bins, of which 22 are the train-null control bins (`results_kappa.json :: catalog`).

All four verify: `protocol_params.json` and `OVERNIGHT_PREDICTION_PROTOCOL.md` against the current tree, and `PROTOCOL.md` and `XUE_LU_PROTOCOL.md` against commit `78f2227`, before the dated copyedit commit `0d8f897` normalised dash characters across the protocol files (see section 5.3). The power audit itself is governed by the K-035 mandates recorded in `HYPOTHESIS_LEDGER.md` (round 2, mandates 1 through 6, plus R2-1(b) and R2-1(c)), with pre-registration commit `05d1e8a`, which predates the run.

### 2.2 The forecasting design whose power is being measured

The design is a train-then-freeze-then-score pipeline. Per-bin tidal-phase modulation is estimated on the training window over 42 eligible 0.4-degree bins; exactly one bin passes the pre-declared selection rule (the Anza bin, p_train = 0.048); its phase is frozen; and that frozen phase is scored on the strictly later 2010 to 2018 test window, alongside a pooled anti-leak control on the 22 bins that were null in training. The reported outcome of that experiment is a null on forecast skill (F-003): the selected bin scored pooled S = -0.100 (anti-aligned) with p = 0.94 and -0.052 bits per event, while the anti-leak control was clean at S = 0.009, p = 0.28. Phase migration did not replicate across catalogues on the same rock in the same decade (QTM phase +25.4 degrees at n = 31 versus SCSN phase +158.7 degrees at n = 49), and walk-forward phase tracking had no skill (S = -0.095, p = 0.92 against a per-year-shift null with p95 = 0.129).

Two statements about that null are binding on this paper and are made here rather than in the discussion. First, the null concerns the forecasting skill of a static susceptibility map, and it does not show that tidal triggering is absent in Southern California. The permitted form of the sentence is the scoped one; the sentence "tidal triggering is null in SoCal" does not appear in this paper. Second, the bin scan does find significant modulation in the San Jacinto and Anza area independently in two catalogues, with elevated but underpowered amplitude at Coso, and the following reconciliation travels with every mention of those detections: Sirorattanakul and Avouac (2026) report no statistically significant semidiurnal tidal modulation anywhere in Southern California on declustered background seismicity above the local completeness magnitude in 5,000-nearest-earthquake neighbourhoods, so our bin-scan detections are not established as a regional feature; the two results are not in contradiction, because they differ in magnitude floor (a fixed M greater than or equal to 1.5 versus their spatially varying local completeness magnitude), in spatial support (0.4-degree bins versus nearest-neighbour clouds), and in statistic (focal-mechanism-resolved phase histogram versus Schuster test); and our own power audit prices the single-bin design at a 25 percent minimum detectable amplitude, which is where a training-window detection of this kind should be expected to live.

### 2.3 The power calculation: injection and recovery through the pipeline

We define the modulation amplitude a as the zero-to-peak fractional amplitude of a sinusoidally modulated seismicity rate, R(t) = R0 [1 + a cos(2 pi t / T)]. This convention matches Sirorattanakul and Avouac's Eq. 4, in which alpha is the half peak-to-peak variation of the rate, so the two amplitudes are directly comparable and neither needs a factor of 2. (Peak-to-trough is 2a in both.)

Synthetic modulation is injected at 11 amplitudes, {0, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.05, 0.07, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8}, with 200 injections per amplitude, into candidate event times drawn from a frozen ETAS process whose parameters are inherited from the program's independently fitted temporal ETAS model (mu = 0.3663, K = 0.03917, alpha_ETAS = 0.5476, c = 0.01008 d, p = 1.0734, M0 = 2.5, b = 0.9349), with only the background rate scaled to densify the candidate pool and with the branching ratio and Omori structure unchanged (`results_k035.json :: etas`). The occupancy total-variation distance between the ETAS candidate-time phase occupancy and uniform is 0.0206, which is the check that the injection substrate does not itself carry a phase preference.

Each injected realisation is passed through the analysis exactly as the real data were, and power is the fraction of realisations rejecting at alpha = 0.05. The minimum detectable amplitude at 80 percent power (MDA80) is obtained by bracketing between adjacent injected amplitudes; every row in the results carries the bracket in which it was determined. Three estimators are scored: the 36-bin phase histogram against orientation-preserving synthetics, the pooled resultant S against circular time shifts, and a Cox intensity likelihood carrying the integrated-lambda0 term.

Six configurations are priced, and they are chosen to span the design rather than to be selected among:

- **A1**, the per-bin training statistic at the selected bin, n = 245;
- **A2**, the complete end-to-end pipeline, 1-of-42 training selection followed by the pooled shift test, n_train = 16,985 and n_test = 3,920;
- **A3**, the pooled statistic over all 42 eligible bins, n = 3,920, which is the design's upper limit;
- **A3b**, the pooled statistic on the 22 train-null control bins, n = 1,906, which is the configuration the original null actually quoted;
- **C**, the Coso Figure 4c replication of Wang et al. (2022), n = 113;
- **D**, the full-catalogue intensity likelihood, n = 23,465.

**The verification that had to pass first.** By mandate, the zero-amplitude arm was run and read before any power arm was read. Its rule is stated in the artifact itself: "the a = 0 arm must hold its false-positive rate before any other arm is read". At a = 0 the empirical rejection rates are 0.070, 0.055, 0.055, 0.065, 0.035, 0.075, 0.050 and 0.020 for the eight phase-histogram configurations, and 0.065, 0.045, 0.055, 0.075 and 0.035 for the five intensity-likelihood configurations. All 13 lie inside the binomial 99 percent acceptance band [0.015, 0.095], and the artifact records `PASS: true` (`results_k035.json :: mandate_4_a0_arm`).

**Null reconstruction check.** The power simulator rebuilds the original experiment's circular-shift null. The reconstruction agrees with the value reported by the original run to floating-point noise on independent shift draws: reported null S p95 = 0.10137774744851648 against reconstructed 0.10137774744851652, and reported null S mean 0.001743733889717438 against reconstructed 0.0017437338897174452 (`results_k035.json :: shift_null_validation`).

### 2.4 The systematics arm and the off-tidal negative controls

Because a powered tidal search is a systematics problem before it is a noise problem, a separate arm injects and models named nuisance lines: S1 at 24.0 h, S2 at 12.0 h, K1 at 23.93447 h, P1 at 24.06589 h, and Msf at 354.36 h, together with an injected S2 detection artifact at 3 percent amplitude, over 100 realisations per cell. Two off-tidal negative-control lines are declared in the protocol, at 11.0 d (264 h) and 16.5 d (396 h). The artifact reports rejection rates for the 11.0 d line only; the 16.5 d line is declared in `results_k035.json :: systematics_R2_1c.off_tidal_control_lines_hours` but no rejection rate for it appears in the results block. **Scope, settled rather than outstanding.** Both lines are declared in the protocol; only the 11.0 d line was scored, and no 16.5 d rejection rate exists in the artifact. Every statement in this paper about off-tidal negative controls is therefore a single-line statement at 11.0 d. Scoring the 16.5 d line would be confirmatory scoring of an already-declared line and is deferred to the ETAS-baseline successor of section 5.4, where it is pre-declared as such and will be reported whichever way it lands.

### 2.5 The clustering correction, kappa

The power simulation treats the events entering each statistic as independent draws from a thinned ETAS intensity. Residual clustering would inflate the variance of the tidal-phase resultant and would make every MDA optimistic. We therefore measure that inflation rather than assume it away.

Define kappa = 1 + 2 sum_{i<j} Cov(u_i, u_j) / sum_i Var(u_i), where u is the (cos, sin) pair of the 36-bin-centre tidal phase and the expectation is taken over the circular-shift ensemble. It is estimated as kappa(T) = 1 + 2 [P_real(T) - mean_j P_jit,j(T)] / sum_i V_i, where P is the sum of unbiased shift-ensemble sample covariances over ordered pairs separated by no more than T, and the reference is a matched-jitter ensemble with J = 6 realisations of uniform jitter over a 30 d window. The primary truncation is T = 10 d, with a reported ladder at 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 30 d, fixed before the run rather than selected from, on 600 shifts with fixed integer seeds. The correction applied to each bound is MDA* = MDA times sqrt(kappa) (`results_kappa.json :: estimator`).

Two earlier estimators were run and are reported rather than hidden. Version 1, the ratio of the rigid-shift variance of the real catalogue to an analytic independent variance, was unbiased but carried 40 to 80 percent relative scatter at every sample size (40 Poisson catalogues gave mean 0.885 with sd 0.406 at n = 200, mean 1.01 with sd 0.482 at n = 1,906, and mean 1.102 with sd 0.78 at n = 20,000) and is rejected. Version 2 was launched, stopped before scoring, and rejected analytically because jitter re-randomises the numerator's chance term rather than cancelling it. No number from either appears in any result.

**The bias audit, with its pass rule declared in advance.** The rule is: |kappa_Poisson - 1| less than or equal to max(0.05, 2 SE) on both density-matched Poisson controls, and |kappa_planted / (3 kappa_base) - 1| less than or equal to 0.05. Two density-matched Poisson controls return kappa = 0.9982 with SE 0.0220 (n = 23,465) and kappa = 0.9314 with SE 0.0559 (n = 1,906) against a truth of 1. A planted 3-times overdispersion is recovered as 2.9502 with SE 0.0413 against its own truth of 2.9866 (three times the base configuration's kappa of 0.99554), a ratio of 0.9878, that is, recovered to 1.2 percent. The artifact records `audit_verdict: PASS`.

**Conservative reading.** The number this paper quotes is not the point estimate. Following the pre-declared rule in `results_kappa.json :: conservative_requote`, kappa_conservative = kappa_hat plus the largest downward Poisson-control offset (0.0686) plus the upper 95 percent half-width, and MDA_conservative = MDA_raw times sqrt(kappa_conservative). This is the number a hostile reviewer should be given, and it is the number in our abstract.

### 2.6 The analytic cross-check

Sirorattanakul and Avouac (2026) express the detectable modulation of a sinusoidally varying seismicity rate R(t) = R0 [1 + alpha cos(2 pi t / T)] through the Schuster random walk P = exp(-D^2 / N), giving alpha = 2 sqrt( -(ln P + 1) / N ) (their Eq. 5), evaluated at a trials-corrected cutoff P95 = 0.05 T / t_catalog that prices the number of cycles of period T contained in the record.

Two conventions travel with any quotation of their number, because omitting either costs a factor of 2 or an outright misreading. First, their alpha is the zero-to-peak fractional amplitude, confirmed from their Eq. 4 and from their own text defining alpha as the ratio of the half peak-to-peak rate variation to the mean rate; peak-to-trough is 2 alpha, and our convention matches theirs. Second, their alpha is the amplitude at which the Schuster statistic's expected value reaches the critical value, which makes it a median detection threshold at approximately 50 percent power and not an 80-percent-power bound. That second fact is what makes the two numbers commensurable, and it is the load-bearing statement of the entire comparison.

We evaluate their closed form at our own sample sizes and at the multiplicity our design actually incurs. Their P95 prices trials over periods; our design tests named tidal lines rather than scanning periods, and our multiplicity is over places, so we assign P = 0.05 / 42 = 1.1905e-3 to A2, the only configuration in which the selection sits inside the statistic, and P = 0.05 to A1, A3, A3b, C and D. Constants: K(P) = -(ln P + 1), so K(0.05) = 1.995732, K(0.05/42) = 5.733402, and alpha = 2 sqrt(K / n). A third column applies their literal period-trials recipe, P = 0.05 T_M2 / 38 yr = 1.8644e-6, giving K = 12.192562, and is reported so that no reviewer can claim we chose the convention that flattered us.

One property of their published thresholds is worth recording because it affects how their number should be read, not ours. Their paper does not state the value of t_catalog used for the gridded analysis. Back-solving their four published thresholds at N = 5,000 (annual 0.038, K2 0.0818, M2 0.0816, N2 0.0815) returns t_catalog = 0.826, 0.796, 0.793 and 0.792 yr respectively, and running forward with t_catalog = 0.8 yr reproduces all four to the printed precision (0.0377, 0.0818, 0.0816, 0.0815). The effective t_catalog is therefore about 0.8 yr, that is, the local time span of the 5,000-nearest-earthquake neighbourhood, and not the 16-year record duration. A corroborating oddity: at the annual period, 0.8 yr implies P95 = 0.0625, which is looser than the nominal 0.05 and is coherent only under the local-window reading. We state this because we quote their thresholds, and because the internal ordering of their three semidiurnal lines (decreasing alpha with increasing period) independently confirms both the square-root form of Eq. 5 and the proportionality of P95 to T. None of it changes any number of ours, because our column evaluates their formula at our own P.

### 2.7 Instrument response and sampling floor: two different estimands, labelled

Two quantities are frequently conflated in tidal-phase work, including once inside this program's own audit trail, and they are kept apart here by construction and by name.

The **instrument response** is what an anchor-based phase convention does to events that are uniform in time. Anchoring trough, ascending zero, peak and descending zero at 0, 90, 180 and 270 degrees and interpolating linearly in time forces each quarter-cycle to occupy exactly 90 degrees of phase, while a mixed diurnal and semidiurnal tide spends unequal time in its quarters. On a deterministic uniform event grid, which removes sampling noise entirely, the measured response is 0.0034 percent for a pure M2 tide and 0.0100 percent for M2 plus S2 (the mechanism check: purely semidiurnal tides should and do give a flat null), and 9.45 percent for a mixed M2 plus S2 plus K1 plus O1 tide. This program's own convention is measured at about 2 percent on Southern-California-like mixes and about 9 percent on diurnal-dominant regimes.

The **Monte Carlo sampling floor** is a property of a simulation's run size and not of the clock: for this statistic the expected amplitude under the null is of order sqrt(pi / N), which is 0.40 percent at N = 200,000. The decisive property is that it decays with run size rather than converging to a bias: the measured semidiurnal control falls from 2.14 percent at N = 12,500 to 0.086 percent at N = 800,000. The two measured endpoints bracket rather than confirm the exact square-root rate, and the rate is not load-bearing for anything in this paper; the companion calibration of section 5.4 will report it on a fixed run-size ladder.

Wherever either quantity appears in this paper it is named. The synthetic-constituent figures quoted above were the state of knowledge when this section was first drafted; the companion calibration described in section 5.4 has since run under its frozen protocol (results_phase_clock_null.json, committed 2026-08-11), and on real modelled tidal series it measures a quarter-anchored deterministic instrument response of 18.39 percent (Coso) and 20.75 percent (Long Valley) at the median, against 0.73 and 0.69 percent for the two-anchor convention, with the Monte Carlo sampling floor reported separately throughout. (Dated update 2026-08-13, replacing a sentence that said the real-series calibration had not yet been computed.) The confirmatory arm of the experiment priced here scores against the series' own phase occupancy and against circular time-shift surrogates, which is the construction that cancels this response, and the program's commit `1ba76f6` records which of its scripts carry that correction and which do not.

### 2.8 Power statements for a transient forcing: the two-branch bracket

Tidal forcing is a transient oscillation, not a permanent Coulomb step. This paper therefore observes a standing rule of the program (S-14(c)) about how any power or sensitivity statement involving such a forcing may be quoted, and the rule matters here because the most natural way to convert our amplitude bound into a physical statement is exactly the conversion the rule forbids.

**The static-step link R = exp(delta_tau / A sigma) is VOID as a power calculator for a transient forcing and is not used anywhere in this paper.** It was retired on our own control data: at a certified 33.8 kPa detection floor it predicts R = 1.25 at the adverse A sigma = 0.15 MPa, against an empirically fitted mean of R = 2.76 on the 30 pre-registered geothermal and volcanic (class A and B) pairs, of 54 source-cell pairs in total, from a pre-registered remote-triggering positive control, a factor of about 2.2 count-weighted, reaching 10 to 200 times per firing cell. This is not evidence against rate-and-state friction, which is correct for what it describes; it is a demonstration that feeding a transient's peak amplitude into a static-step link is a category error.

The replacement is a bracket over functional forms, with the branch that governs chosen to be the one adverse to the claim being made:

- **Branch S (static-equivalent, pessimistic response):** R = exp(delta_tau / A sigma) over the bracket A sigma in {0.03, 0.10, 0.15} MPa, retained only as a lower bound on response. It may not be used to declare that any analysis had no power.
- **Branch E (empirical, optimistic response):** the response-versus-peak-dynamic-stress relation fitted from the 30 pre-registered geothermal and volcanic (class A and B) pairs, of 54 source-cell pairs in total, R(sigma) = exp(-1.846) sigma_kPa^0.812, with a cluster-bootstrap 95 percent interval on the exponent of [0.52, 1.65] over four source clusters, on a support of [7.1, 346.9] kPa. Because a power law with exponent 0.81 is flatter than an exponential, the branches cross near sigma of 15 to 20 kPa, so neither branch is uniformly adverse and the ends must be assigned per statement.

**The two sentences this paper is licensed to write, and the one it is not.**

> *Bound branch.* Quoted on Branch S, the pessimistic response: the design priced here resolves a seismicity-rate modulation of 6.6 percent (pooled, conservative) at 80 percent power, and a rate-state static-step link evaluated over A sigma in {0.03, 0.10, 0.15} MPa places a lower bound on the response expected from a 0.6 kPa semidiurnal tidal stress amplitude that is far below that resolution at every bracket end. A bound must not assume the generous link, so this is the branch on which our non-detection is quoted.

> *Feasibility branch.* Quoted on Branch E, the optimistic response: for a receiver class in which Branch E has been measured, a 0.6 kPa transient is outside the fitted support of [7.1, 346.9] kPa, so Branch E is an extrapolation and not a measurement here. For the receiver class of this study (a broad Southern California catalogue at M greater than or equal to 1.5, not the pre-registered geothermal and volcanic cells on which Branch E was fitted) **Branch E is UNMEASURED, not amplified.** Quoting a geothermal amplification at a non-geothermal receiver is the single most likely way to turn a by-product into a false claim, and we do not do it.

> *Consequence, stated in the headline rather than the limitations, because the width of the bracket is a finding about our own ignorance:* any statement converting our amplitude bound into a bound on the stress sensitivity A sigma is **POWER-INDETERMINATE** under this rule, and this paper prints the bracket instead of a single number. We note for completeness that Sirorattanakul and Avouac (2026) do report such a corollary, A sigma greater than 7.5 kPa via their Eq. 11. We neither adopt nor dispute it; our own standard forbids us from competing on that quantity with a single-branch number, and we say so rather than quietly omitting the comparison.

### 3.2 The clustering correction went the other way from our prior

The program's prior expectation, recorded before the measurement, was that every bound in Table 1 was optimistic by an unmeasured factor. It is not. Kappa is consistent with 1 in all six configurations, spanning 0.9165 to 1.0303, with the largest value at the smallest sample (C, n = 113). The correction is therefore of order 1 to 6 percent rather than a factor, and its sign is mostly against us in the sense that matters least: kappa is below 1 in five of the six configurations, so five of the six point-estimate bounds move slightly downward, and only C, the smallest sample, moves up. The pooled design limit moves from 6.29 percent to 6.02 percent at the point estimate and to 6.59 percent in the deliberately adversarial conservative reading, and stays in single digits either way. The conservative reading, which is what this paper quotes, moves every row upward by construction, because it adds the largest downward Poisson-control offset and the upper interval half-width before taking the square root.

This is an informative null on kappa and not an underpowered one, and the audit establishes that directly. A planted 3-times overdispersion is recovered to 1.2 percent, so the estimator can see inflation when it is there. A true kappa of 1.10 would have resolved at better than 2 standard errors in four configurations (A1, C, A3b and D, whose standard errors are all below 0.05), and in all six when measured as the distance from each configuration's own estimate, and kappa = 1.5 would have been unmissable everywhere.

### 3.3 The estimator comparison, which did not deliver what we hoped

The Cox intensity likelihood is not materially more powerful than the phase histogram at matched sample size. At the full catalogue it reaches MDA80 = 2.83 percent against the histogram's 2.93 percent; pooled over all eligible bins it reaches 7.21 percent against 7.31 percent (`results_k035.json :: configs`). The gain from moving to a likelihood formulation is about 3 percent in amplitude, not a factor. We report this because the program expected otherwise, and because anyone considering the same substitution should be able to price it before spending the effort.

### 3.4 The control failure, reported in the headline

**This is the result we most want a reader to see, and it works against our largest number.**

At full-catalogue n the off-tidal negative-control line at 11.0 d rejects at 0.34 against a nominal 0.05, with an upper acceptance bound for a nominal 0.05 rate of 0.11. The same line holds at n = 1,906, rejecting at 0.06. Under the injected S2 artifact the full-catalogue failure worsens (0.40 at a = 0 and 0.37 at a = 0.01) while the smaller configuration continues to hold (0.07 and 0.05). The tidal-band lines themselves remained calibrated throughout, in the zero-amplitude arm and under the systematics arm (`results_k035.json :: systematics_R2_1c`).

The mechanism is not mysterious and it is not ours. ETAS clustering deposits coherent power at a control period well beyond the aftershock decay time faster than the statistical error shrinks, which is the drifting-p-value signature that Ader and Avouac (2013) documented and that motivated their construction of the Schuster spectrum in the first place. Our off-tidal control is a one-point special case of their instrument: two lines were declared and one was scored, and we say so rather than presenting our failure as an unexplained anomaly.

**The consequence, stated as a restriction on ourselves.** This bounds the method and not the tidal result, but the 2.83 to 2.95 percent full-catalogue bound is not safe to quote as a stand-alone upper limit until the test conditions on an ETAS baseline rather than on a stationary background rate. The number this paper quotes is therefore the pooled 6.6 percent figure, whose control line holds, and not the tighter full-catalogue figure.

### 3.6 The two routes agree, and they disagree by approximately the amount they should

Evaluating the analytic closed form at our sample sizes and multiplicity gives the column in Table 1. Every row agrees within a factor of 1.65, and every ratio has the same sign: 1.22 to 1.56 against our point estimates, 1.31 to 1.65 against our conservative ones.

The offset is not slop. For the Schuster construction the test statistic is 2R^2/N on two degrees of freedom, with noncentrality lambda = N a^2 / 2. At P = 0.05 the 50-percent-power noncentrality, at which the statistic's expected value reaches the critical value, is lambda_50 = 2 (c - 1) = 3.99 with c = ln(1/P), while 80 percent power requires lambda_80 = 9.63, so the amplitude ratio between the two power levels is sqrt(9.63 / 3.99) = 1.55. At P = 0.05 / 42 the same construction gives 1.29. Every observed ratio lies between 1.22 and 1.56 against this prediction, that is, within 21 percent on the loosest row and within 1 percent on the closest, which is the size and the sign the 50-percent-to-80-percent conversion requires and is not reproduced by any other single factor we have identified. We do not offer a row-level account of the residual. The two configurations closest to prediction, C at 1.56 and D at 1.52, and the two furthest, A3b at 1.23 and A2 at 1.22, do not separate cleanly by null construction: A1, whose null is orientation-preserving synthetics rather than circular shifts (`results_kappa.json :: requoted_bounds`, field `null_carried_clustering`), sits at 1.33 alongside the circular-shift rows. We report the residual rather than explain it.

**The column under which we look worse, volunteered.** Under the literal period-trials multiplicity convention applied over a 38-year record, our pooled bound would be 11.15 percent rather than 6.6 percent, which is looser than the 8.16 percent published for California. We do not adopt that convention, because we did not scan periods, but the column is shown and the reason is given in one sentence rather than left for a reviewer to reconstruct.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 The objection this paper expects, answered before it is raised

The citation to pre-empt is Sirorattanakul and Avouac (2026): same region, same year, a higher-profile venue, broader catalogue coverage, and it already contains an amplitude bound. The objection arrives in roughly this form: *Sirorattanakul and Avouac analysed all of California, reported no significant semidiurnal modulation, and bounded the amplitude at 8.16 percent with a closed-form expression, no Monte Carlo, and a multiplicity correction. You ran a large simulation to get 6.6 percent for a subset of the same region. What is the contribution?*

Their threshold is the amplitude at which the Schuster p-value crosses the significance cutoff, which is a detection threshold at approximately even odds and not a bound at a stated power, and their multiplicity correction prices the number of trial periods rather than the number of trial places. Our design selects one bin out of forty-two on training data and reports it, so the quantity a reader needs is the modulation that this pipeline, selection included, would recover 80 percent of the time. We obtain it by injection through the full pipeline, after verifying that the simulator's false-positive rate at zero injected amplitude lies inside a binomial 99 percent band in 13 of 13 configurations. Evaluating their closed form at our sample sizes is reported alongside ours, so the two methods are compared rather than asserted against each other. The two routes agree to within a factor of 1.7 on every configuration, and the residual, median 1.4 and 1.52 to 1.56 on the two closest configurations, is quantitatively the 50-percent-to-80-percent power conversion factor of 1.55 expected for a Schuster statistic. The methods do not merely agree: they disagree by approximately the amount their differing definitions require.

The second objection is our own control failure at large n. We found it, we report it in the results rather than the limitations, it is the reason our quotable number is 6.6 percent and not 3.0 percent, and Ader and Avouac (2013) already named the mechanism.

The third objection is Beeler and Lockner's own sample-size statement. Their abstract-level remark that detection of the predicted response would require more than 13,000 earthquakes sits in genuine tension with our result, because our n = 23,465 configuration is above that threshold and is still bounded only at about 3.0 percent, and even that number is withheld here on control-failure grounds. The reconciliation we offer is that a raw event count is not a sufficient statistic for detectability once declustering, focal-mechanism matching, cell selection and residual clustering are priced, which is the general argument this paper is making. **TODO-VERIFY: the ">13,000 earthquakes to detect" quotation is currently sourced at abstract level and is recorded as UNVERIFIED in the program's own dossier. It must be checked against the article PDF before submission, because a reviewer who knows that abstract will raise it.**

### 5.2 What this paper does not show

1. It does not bound the physical tidal response of the Southern California crust. It bounds what this design at these sample sizes could have recovered. Larger catalogues exist and would move these numbers.
2. It does not show that tidal triggering is absent in Southern California, and the sentence asserting that does not appear here.
3. The full-catalogue configuration's off-tidal negative control fails, so the 2.83 to 2.95 percent figure is reported but not quoted as a stand-alone upper limit (section 3.4).
4. Kappa prices the tidal-phase-coherent temporal pair covariance, truncated at T = 10 d with a ladder to 30 d. It does not price spatial or magnitude clustering effects on the bin-selection step, and that gap is open.
5. The intensity likelihood is evaluated on the same 36 phase bins as the histogram, so the estimator comparison in section 3.3 isolates the estimator and not the binning.
6. The bounds are computed against a phase clock whose own null response has been measured at about 2 percent for our convention on Southern-California-like mixes, on synthetic tides only (section 2.7). A 2 percent instrument response beneath a 6.6 percent bound is not fatal, but it is not negligible either, and **the bound must be restated once that calibration is run on real tidal series.** This is the single most important open coupling in the work.
7. It does not include the centroid-versus-origin location sensitivity that Bradley and Hubbard (2024) showed matters for Cochran et al. (2004). The sensitivity arises because a hypocentre-to-centroid offset shifts the assigned tidal phase, and its magnitude scales with rupture duration, so it is expected to be small for the magnitude range analysed here. We have not quantified it on our catalogue, and we do not claim it is negligible; it is an uncorrected term of unmeasured size beneath every bound in Table 1.
8. It is not a new kind of object. Ader and Avouac (2013) bounded Nepal below 18 percent and Sirorattanakul and Avouac (2026) bounded California below 8.16 percent four months ahead of this work. Ours is tighter and is measured at a stated power through a simulated selection.

### 5.4 What we would do next, and what would falsify this

The immediate methodological successor is a powered full-catalogue test that conditions on an ETAS baseline rather than on a stationary background rate, which is the fix that the 11.0 d control failure demands and which would, if it works, make the 3 percent configuration quotable. The immediate calibration successor is to push real tidal stress series, rather than synthetic constituent sums, through the phase-clock response measurement of section 2.7, for at minimum the Coso series, a diurnal-dominant Pacific-margin site, and, if the authors are willing, a published study's own series. A companion scoring upgrade would add a Molchan error diagram and a probability gain to the underlying forecast-skill result, so that it is directly commensurable with Hirose et al. (2022).

The claim of this paper is falsifiable in a specific and cheap way: re-run the injection at any of the six configurations, at the stated amplitudes, against the frozen protocol and the published code, and check both that the zero-amplitude arm lands inside the binomial band and that the 80-percent-power crossing lands where Table 1 says it does. A demonstration that the injection substrate carries a phase preference, or that the recovery step is optimistic, would move every number in the table.

## 6. Attribution and prior art

**This section is placed before Data Availability deliberately, and it names the prior work before this paper's own contribution is restated.**

The standing California tidal bound belongs to **Sirorattanakul and Avouac (2026)**, who report no statistically significant semidiurnal tidal modulation in either Northern or Southern California and bound the zero-to-peak amplitude below 8.16 percent of the background rate using the closed form alpha = 2 sqrt( -(ln P + 1) / N ) with a multiplicity scaling over trial periods. Their two conventions travel with every quotation of their number in this paper: their alpha is zero-to-peak, and it is a median detection threshold at approximately 50 percent power rather than a bound at a stated power. This work does not displace that bound; it prices a different and narrower design, at a stated power, on an overlapping region.

The closed-form minimum-detectable-amplitude lineage, and the Schuster spectrum, belong to **Ader and Avouac (2013)**, who also published the explanation of the control-line behaviour we report in section 3.4: p-values drift at periods larger than the decay time of aftershocks, which is why they built a spectrum rather than testing isolated periods. Our single scored off-tidal control line is a one-point special case of their instrument.

The theory line this paper measures against is **Beeler and Lockner (2003)**'s prediction of an order-1-percent seismicity-rate modulation at tidal stressing amplitudes, together with their statement of the sample-size consequence.

The structural precedent for a gridded tidal-cell scan, and the diagnosis of grid-selection sensitivity that motivates computing power through the selection step, is **Wang and Shearer (2015)**, who found low-p tidal cells in central Japan no more numerous than chance. The trials-factor formalism we implement by simulation rather than analytically is **Gross and Vitells (2010)**. The Coso Figure 4c configuration priced in Table 1 is from **Wang, Shearer, Vidale, Xu, Trugman and Fialko (2022)**. The declustering tradition underlying the kappa concern is **Reasenberg (1985)**'s. The one prior scored tidal forecast model is **Hirose, Maeda and Kamigaichi (2022)**, and the forecast-skill null underlying this paper is a rediscovery in a new geometry with respect to it, complementary and not novel. The informal Southern California bound that this line of work formalises is **Vidale, Agnew, Johnston and Oppenheimer (1998)**'s. The public examination of the replication status of tidal-triggering claims, including the centroid-versus-origin sensitivity, is **Bradley and Hubbard (2024)**, which we cite as a DOI-bearing but not peer-reviewed replication essay. The rate-and-state formulation whose static-step link we decline to use as a power calculator for a transient forcing is **Dieterich (1994)**'s, and section 2.8 is not evidence against it.

The catalogues and the stress-resolution convention are from **Lu, Xue, Yue, Zhuang and Zhao (2025)**, section 2.5, with data from SCEDC, the Yang-Hauksson-Shearer focal-mechanism catalogue, NCEDC and NCSS, and geodetic velocities from NGL MIDAS. The null being priced in this paper is our own.

**The single novelty statement this paper makes, and it is the only one.**

> Minimum detectable amplitudes for periodic seismicity modulation have been reported analytically (Ader & Avouac, 2013; Sirorattanakul & Avouac, 2026), and the sensitivity of tidal p-values to the choice of spatiotemporal cell has been documented (Wang & Shearer, 2015). To our knowledge the present study is the first to combine the two: to compute the minimum detectable modulation at a stated statistical power by simulating the full analysis pipeline, *including the data-driven selection of the reported cell*, and to verify the simulator's false-positive rate at zero injected amplitude before reading any power result.

**The basis of that statement, recorded so a reader can price it.** The claim is one of absence in the prior literature, and absences are only as good as the search behind them. It rests on two things. The first is a structured adversarial prior-art search across five sweeps, whose negative results and whose access failures are both on the record: ScienceDirect returned 403 for Ader and Avouac (2013), Wiley returned 403 for Lu et al. (2025) and for Wang and Shearer (2015), and science.org returned 403 for both *Science Advances* papers, of which the 2026 one was recovered through PubMed Central and one further item was not recovered and is listed unverified. The second, and the one that carries the weight, is a full-text human read of Sirorattanakul and Avouac's Materials and Methods performed on 2026-08-12, which confirmed that their 8.16 percent threshold is the analytic Schuster detection threshold at N = 5,000 with a trials-scaled cutoff, that it is a 95-percent-confidence detection threshold rather than an 80-percent-power minimum detectable amplitude, and that it is not propagated through their selection pipeline (declustering, completeness filtering, and 5,000-nearest-event grid selection) by injection-recovery or by any power simulation; their declustering quality is validated a posteriori only. Had that read gone the other way, the sentence above would have been withdrawn rather than qualified.

**Forbidden forms, observed.** The phrases "the first powered upper bound on tidal modulation", "the reference bound in this literature", and "bounds, not nulls" used as a bare slogan implying the framing is ours, do not appear in this manuscript and may not be added to it.
